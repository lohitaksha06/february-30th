# FEBRUARY 30th — Plot

> **Sad horror, with something chasing you.**
>
> Every monster in this game is a disappointed child. Nothing can be killed.

---

## 1. Before you start

The game opens on a black screen with a name field, and under it:

```
   ┌──────────┐   ┌──────────┐
   │   BOY    │   │   GIRL   │
   └──────────┘   └──────────┘
```

Pick one. That's the whole character creator. You look at exactly one thing — a
stylised child, near-silhouette, in a room with slightly too much light — and
then you are in your bedroom.

**You do not get a name.** Nobody says it out loud. You are the player.

**They do.**

---

## 2. The two of you

### You

Eleven. You fell asleep in your clothes because the party ran late and you were
too tired to change, which is a very specific kind of eleven-year-old.

**You notice things.** That's your one trait and the whole game runs on it. You
remember what people said, you count things without meaning to, you know there
were nine guests and not ten. It's not clever and it's not creepy — it's just what
being you is like, and it's why the 30th can talk to you and why you can survive
in there.

You are also **polite**. You open the wardrobe because it would be rude not to.
You apologise to things that are not alive. That is your entire defence mechanism
and it is not enough.

### Wren

**That is their name and you learn it in the first two minutes.**

Wren is eleven. Wren has your face, which is the first thing that unsettles you
and it should not be the thing that does.

Wren is not sad. **Wren is annoying, and that is on purpose.** Wren talks
constantly. Wren asks questions nobody asked for. Wren wants to know how long
until morning, then wants to know if you think the fridge makes a noise on
purpose, then points out that a moth is a bit stupid for flying at a light that
isn't warm.

Wren has been in the wardrobe a long time and has clearly had a lot of time to
think about moths.

**This is the whole reason the death works.** You cannot mourn someone who only
ever seemed sad. You have to have been annoyed by them first. Wren tells you the
moth thing and you think *that's daft* and that is the moment they become
someone.

They ask you to promise something in the first minute and it is a small promise,
and you say yes, and **the game never lets you off the hook for it.**

---

## 3. Why there's a 30th

A calendar is a machine with too many days and not enough boxes. Once every four
years it gets an extra day, and it has to either **keep it** (February 29th) or
**throw it away** (February 30th).

**February 30th is the discard day.**

But you can only throw away a day if it's **empty**. And once a year the world
produces a surplus birthday, because leap-day birthdays are the one kind the world
reliably forgets. No card in the shop. Schools get the form wrong. The date is
hard to say out loud.

So every birthday that goes unacknowledged gets pushed into the discard day.

**February 30th is a landfill made of forgotten birthdays — and it is completely
full.**

That is where the ghouls are, and that is why it's frightening: it's the most
crowded day there has ever been, and all of it is children.

---

## 4. Your mother

**She got everything right this year.** That's the change and it's the whole
point.

- The cake is the right cake, not a shop one
- **Eleven candles**, and she counted them twice
- The name on it is spelled correctly, in the good icing pen
- The right song. She knows the words to all of it.
- She put the good plates out, the ones that only come out for people
- She stayed up past midnight, which she never does, and she is still awake in the
  kitchen when you wake up

She did not forget. She got it right. She tried.

**And there is still a 30th.**

That's the horror. A perfect birthday is not enough. She could have done
everything — and she did do everything — and it did not matter, because the thing
that needs to happen isn't a cake. It's somebody being *counted*.

Wren's whole existence is the question, asked over and over: *was I counted?*
And no one ever said yes.

---

## 5. The ghouls

When the 30th had to fit everything into one day it could only keep one child
whole. It kept Wren. **The ones it squeezed out became ghouls.**

A ghoul is a child's shape taken too far. Head too large. One arm longer than the
other. Elbows bending the wrong direction. A face pressed flat, and no eyes drawn
on it at all — the texture just doesn't have eyes, and it is worse than anything
you could draw. Still wearing a party hat two sizes too small.

They aren't evil. They're **crushed**, and they're still waiting to be called in
for a birthday that happened a long time ago.

**They hunt by sound.** Running is loud. Walking is quiet. A closed door stops
them. Light pushes them back, but the flashlight is also loud, and every time you
turn it on you have chosen.

**You cannot kill anything.** You can say a dead child's birthday out loud, once,
at close range, and it stops for about eight seconds and then it remembers where
you are. That's the whole combat system of this game.

Their sound is not a roar. It's **a bad cheer** — children clapping and cheering,
slightly out of time with themselves, doubling from one voice to four to six.
You are being hunted by something that is nearly a birthday party.

---

## 6. The plot

### ACT I — 29TH STREET

You come through the wardrobe into your own street, except every mailbox has your
house number on it and the party ended hours ago and nobody will admit it happened.

Balloons deflating upward. Adults standing motionless in the gardens, facing the
street. They don't turn. They notice you, and that's all, and you can't work out
whether being noticed is dangerous or just *known*.

Wren walks beside you the whole time talking about the moths, and you realise
after a while that you can't remember when they stopped talking, and that you're
afraid of the moment they do.

You find your house. **The cake is still on the table.** The candles have burned
down to stubs and they're still lit — four hours, which is not possible.

And there's a **photograph** on the table. Wren is in it. Wren is standing next
to another child with your face, and that child is not you, and Wren never
mentions it.

Wren tells you the rule about the Guests, kindly, like they're being helpful:

> *"They don't chase. There's no chase. They just notice you."*

Then, at the end of the street, **a ghoul comes round the corner**, and the game
does not explain it, and you run. This is the safest place it could possibly
happen and the game still will not hold your hand.

Wren is with you when you stop. This is the last time for a while.

### ACT II — THE LONG WAY

The road. Cars going the other way, all of them full, everyone facing forward,
**one passenger too many in every back seat.** It is in every single car and it is
never explained.

There's a bus at the side of the road with its interior light on. Your mother is
driving it. She doesn't look at you. She says:

> *"We'll be there soon."*

The radio is playing tomorrow's news, backwards, and one word comes through
clearly, forwards: **your name.**

Then you have to run again — there's a ghoul on the road, and this one is faster
than the last one, and the road is long and open with no doors.

**And Wren is not with you when you stop.**

There is a party hat on the tarmac. That's all that's there.

You were doing the right thing. You were outrunning it. That's the whole reason
you're standing here alone.

**The objective changes.** You were looking for a way out. Now you're looking for
them, in a world that's looking for you.

### ACT III — LOST AND FOUND

A cloakroom of coats nobody collected, and it's much worse now that there's nobody
talking.

There are **twelve children with your face**, all standing between the racks,
waiting to be collected by an adult who isn't coming. Exactly one of them is Wren
and you have to work out which.

You check them against what you know. The tooth. The cowlick. The scar on the
left knee from the bike in the summer. The way they stand when they're nervous.

**Getting it wrong is not death.** It's a scream, and the room resets, and the game
never shows you what happened and never tells you.

You find Wren. Wren is not pleased about this. Wren says, *"You took ages,"* and
that is the whole reunion and it is perfect.

Wren finds their coat. The name tag reads their name with **one letter wrong.**

And this is where the game finally tells you what Wren is, in one line, in a
cloakroom, with no ceremony:

> *"There's not enough room in here for me."*
>
> *"There hasn't been for a long time."*

### ACT IV — THE AFTER

A waiting room that is never called. The only quiet act, and the relief is worse
than the fear.

Six objects belonging to six different children. You put their days back together.
The answer is that there is no answer — six separate ordinary nights where six
separate adults each forgot a different child's birthday. No mastermind. No
tragedy. Just six small failures, in six different years.

**Wren is in the pile.** Not one child. Every forgotten birthday in the calendar,
squashed into one shape, because the 30th could only think of one child to pour
them into. The ghouls are what got left over.

Wren asks you what happens after the party.

You can tell them the truth or you can lie. **Both stay available. The game never
locks either one.**

> *"Will I still be there in the morning?"*

Wren thinks about it for a second. Then:

> *"That's alright. As long as it's the right morning."*

And then the room turns into your kitchen, and your mother is there, and Wren is
sitting at the table, and the two of them say happy birthday to somebody who is
not in the room yet.

Your mother looks right at you and does not see you. That is not a ghost trick.
She is just looking at Wren.

### ACT V — THE 30TH

A cathedral made of printed calendar pages. February in red. The 30th printed and
still wet, and you can watch it being printed by something you can't find.

**It's full.** Four hundred children, all having a birthday, all with your face.
The crowd Wren was made of, celebrating all at once because there was no other way
to hold them.

They aren't monsters. That's the horror. They're children, having a birthday,
simultaneously, in a room with no room in it.

To make the 30th a real day, one real February day has to stop being real. The
only one available is the 29th — your birthday, the one your mother got right.

You offer it.

Wren asks if you're sure. **Three times.** Not because they don't want it. Because
they've been on the receiving end of someone deciding something for them and they
want to be sure this one's actually yours to give.

You say yes three times.

### The party

It goes small and warm. Not the cathedral — your kitchen. The same table. The same
cake. Eleven candles.

**And the name on the cake is misspelled — one letter. The same letter as the coat
in Act III.** Nobody could get it right. Wren doesn't notice.

> *"Is this the real one?"*
>
> *"Yeah. This is it."*

Wren blows out the candles. Wren is very happy. Wren is **talking again** — about
whether candles count as wishes if nobody's listening, about the moths, about
whether your mother is always this nice.

You say *"Happy birthday, Wren."*

It is the only time anyone says their name in the whole game.

### The death

**Wren doesn't fall down.** No gasp, no blood, nothing to clean up. **Wren gets
less.**

The party was paid for out of them. They were the only one who stayed whole long
enough to hold the whole crowd for one night, and a real day has to end, and when
it ends they all go home, and they take the part of Wren that was them.

Wren isn't frightened. Wren isn't in pain. **Wren isn't even sad.** They look
completely present and completely calm, and that's the detail that breaks people.

> *"Did I do it right?"*

And you:

> *"Yeah. You did."*

Wren smiles. There's a party hat on the table that isn't their size.

**The music box stops. First silence in the entire game.**

### The morning

You wake up in your own bed. It's the 29th. Your mother is in the kitchen
calling you down, because it is your birthday and she remembered, and she got
everything right.

The party is still on the table. Nobody cleaned up — she's been asleep.

**Two hats. Two plates. She put out the good plates.**

She doesn't know why. She'll say she doesn't remember doing it, and by next week
there will be one hat and she won't know that there was ever two.

You remember. That's the whole ending. Wren's gone, something's left behind, and
you're the only person alive who knows it was a person.

Every fourth year, at midnight, one knock. Never explained. Never opened. Never
stopped listening for it.

---

## 7. Why it's sad horror

**Sad:** every monster is a child who got let down. You spend the game running
from things you feel sorry for, and the only way past one of them is to say its
birthday out loud, which costs you a piece of your own. Wren isn't a quest. Wren
is someone you got annoyed by and then loved, and they die in the kitchen while
talking about candles.

**Horror:** four hundred faceless children having a birthday at once. One extra
passenger in every car. Adults going through the motions at a party that isn't
happening. A coat that fits.

**And the real one:** your mother did everything right and it wasn't enough. She
counted the candles twice and she sang the whole song and she stayed up past
midnight, and there is still a 30th, because the thing that was missing was never
the cake.

**Nothing can be killed.** The only verbs are **run, hide, and be kind**, and being
kind is the expensive one.

---

## 8. Open questions

1. **Is "Wren" the right name?** It's gender-neutral so it survives the boy/girl
   choice, which is exactly what the mirroring needs. But it's a real call.
2. **Is the moth thing too much?** It's 20 seconds of dialogue and it's the whole
   reason the death works. If it feels cheap we can find another way to make Wren
   annoying.
3. **Does saying Wren's name once carry enough?** It's currently the only time
   anyone says it. That may be right.
