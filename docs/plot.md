# FEBRUARY 30th — Plot (v4)

> **Sad horror with something chasing you.**
>
> Three changes from v3: you **create your character**, the 30th contains **ghouls
> that hunt you**, and the Boy is **taken from you** in Act II — which turns the
> game from a descent into a rescue.

---

## 1. Before you start

The game opens on a black screen with a name field, and under it:

```
   ┌──────────┐   ┌──────────┐
   │   BOY    │   │   GIRL   │
   └──────────┘   └──────────┘
```

Pick one. That is the whole character creator.

**You look at exactly one thing** — a stylised child, near-silhouette, in a
room with too much light — and then you are in your bedroom.

**The choice is not cosmetic.** It is the entire premise. The child in the
cupboard has *your* face. Choose a girl and the thing waiting for you in the dark
is a girl, and every ghost in the 30th wearing your face is a girl, and the four
hundred children at the end of the world are all *you*.

You do not get a name. Nobody ever says it out loud.

**Then there are twelve minutes of house.**

> **The full prologue script — every beat, every line, every sound — is in
> [`prologue.md`](prologue.md).** Wake at 11:48. Find the cake. Go back to bed.
> The clock turns to 11:59 and the music box stops mid-bar. Three knocks. The
> wardrobe. Your mother unlocking the door. One input: push them in.

---

## 2. Premise

You are eleven. Tonight is **February 29th** — the rarest birthday there is.

Your mother has never got it right. The 27th one year, the 1st the next, "the
last Friday of February" the year after. This year she finally did: a cake on the
table, eleven candles, the correct number on it.

At midnight you wake to knocking from the wardrobe under your window. Three knocks.
A pause. Three knocks.

Inside is a child with your face. Your cowlick. The gap where your front right
tooth used to be. They are wearing clothes that are almost yours and specifically
not — a party hat two sizes too small, a shirt buttoned inside-out, a jacket cut
uneven at the hem.

**Then your mother unlocks the door downstairs** and you have about four seconds,
and the only option available to you is to push them further in to hide them.

You fall in together.

> *"It's my birthday too. It's the thirtieth."*
>
> *"There's no thirtieth."*
>
> *"Not down here."*

You open the door to look at them properly. The door is nine feet tall and the
inside of the cupboard is a hallway.

They follow you out. They tell you the whole of what they want:

> *"I want a party."*
>
> *"That's it. That's the whole of it. I just want a party."*

---

## 3. Why there is a 30th

A calendar is a machine with too many days and not enough boxes. Once every four
years it gets an extra day. You either **keep it** (February 29th) or **throw it
away** (February 30th).

**February 30th is the discard day.**

A day can only be discarded if it is empty. Once a year the world produces a
surplus birthday, because leap-day birthdays are the one kind the world reliably
forgets. So every unacknowledged birthday goes in the discard bin.

**The 30th is a landfill made of forgotten birthdays — and it is over-full.**

Every cancelled party. Every child told "next year" four years running. Every kid
collected too late. All of it, one day, no room.

And that is where the ghouls come from.

---

## 4. The ghouls

> **The ghouls are the children the Boy was made of — the fragments that got
> left behind when he was chosen to be the one.**

The 30th had to compress four thousand forgotten children into a single shape,
and it could only keep one. It kept him, and it squeezed the rest until they
stopped being people.

A ghoul is what is left: a child's shape taken too far. The head is too large.
One arm is longer than the other. The joints bend a direction they shouldn't. The
face has been squeezed flat, and it is still wearing a party hat two sizes too
small.

They are not evil. They are **crushed**, and they are still waiting to be called
in for a birthday that happened years ago and will never happen now.

### How they hunt

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **They hunt sound** | Running is loud. Crouch-walking is quiet. Sprinting behind you is the worst thing you can do. |
| **They lose interest** | Break line of sight *and* go quiet for long enough and they wander off |
| **They do not open doors you have closed** | A closed door is a real, physical, safe thing. Use them. |
| **They cannot be killed** | There is no weapon in this game and there never will be |
| **They are terrified of light** | They have been left in a day that had no room for them. Light pushes them back. |
| **You can stop one, briefly, by saying its birthday** | At close range. It works once per ghoul. It costs you a memory. |

That last rule is the mechanic the whole game is built on. **Compassion is the
only weapon and it is disposable.**

### What they sound like

Not a roar. A **bad cheer** — a party noise played slightly wrong, from too
many children at once, clapping out of time. It starts as one voice and doubles
into four, and then six, and it is coming down a hallway.

The horror is that it is recognisably **people enjoying themselves.**

---

## 5. The Unacknowledged

The quiet ones. Ghosts of *specific* forgotten children — the individuals, where
ghouls are the crushed fragments and the Boy is the shape that survived.

They flicker: solid peripherally, gone head-on.

> **"Is this my birthday?"**

**"Yes"** → they accept it, go quiet, and are gone.
**"No"**, silence, or running → they take it. You lose a piece of your own
birthday.

You cannot help everyone. There are never enough of you to go around, and the
game never tells you that ignoring them all would have been fine.

---

## 6. Your mother

**The only person who ever remembered your birthday**, and this year she finally
got it right. She is why the 29th exists as a day you can spend.

Three appearances, no cutscene:

| Act | Where | What she does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | A garden, among the motionless Guests, facing the street | Nothing. Most players miss her. |
| II | Driving a car, the one that takes them | *"We'll be there soon."* Does not look at you. |
| IV | Your kitchen table | Both of you say *happy birthday* to nobody. |

**She is one floor away for the whole game and you never rescue her.**

## 7. The plot

### ACT I — 29TH STREET · the party that ended

Your street. Every mailbox has your house number. The party ended hours ago and
nobody will admit it happened. Balloons deflating *upward*. Adults standing
motionless, facing the street.

Your companion walks beside you, talking, because they are pleased to have
someone.

**First ghoul at the end of the street.** Nothing explained — it comes round a
corner and you run. The game teaches you here, in the safest place it possibly
could, that the 30th has teeth: ghouls, crouch-walking, closed doors, and a
flashlight that pushes them back.

Then the Unacknowledged start appearing, asking about their birthdays, and you
learn the second exchange.

You find the cake. Still there. Eleven candles, correct number, name spelled
right.

> **Ends with** you and your companion deciding to go and look for a way out —
> and a bad cheer from somewhere you cannot place.

### ACT II — THE LONG WAY · **he is taken from you**

The road. Cars going the other way, all full, everyone facing forward, **one
passenger too many in every back seat.** It is never explained.

You get into one. The driver is your mother. She does not look at you. *"We'll
be there soon."* The radio plays tomorrow's news backwards and one word comes
through clearly, forwards: your name.

**And then they take your companion.**

It is fast and it is not your fault and you do not see it clearly. You were
running. You were doing the right thing — you were outrunning a ghoul — and
when you stop, the street is empty and your companion is not on it.

There is a party hat on the tarmac. There is no one else.

**You are alone for the first time in the game and the objective has changed.**
You were looking for a way out. Now you are looking for them, in a world that is
actively looking for you.

### ACT III — LOST AND FOUND · alone, and hunted

A cloakroom of coats nobody collected, and it is much worse now.

**The Late** — children who will not leave a waiting room. They do not chase. They
**wait**, patiently, and the longer you look the less like children they are.

Twelve children with your face, all waiting to be collected. One of them is your
companion. You find them by the tooth, the cowlick, the scar.

**Wrong answer is not death** — it is a scream and a reset, and the game never
shows or explains what happened to the one you chose.

The coat that fits you has a name tag with **one letter wrong.**

**And the whole act is a hunt.** Two ghouls roam the cloakroom. They are faster
than Act I's. The hall is a loop of racks and the only way through is to keep
moving.

### ACT IV — THE AFTER · where you find out what they are

A waiting room that is never called. The only act with nothing chasing you, and
the relief is worse than the fear.

**The Almost** — translucent adults going through the motions of a party that is
not happening. Not a threat. The people who almost came.

Six objects belonging to six different children. You put their days back
together. The answer is that there is no answer: six separate ordinary nights
where six separate adults each forgot a different child's birthday.

**And your companion is in the pile.** Not one child — every forgotten birthday in
the calendar, compressed into one shape, because the 30th could only think of one
child to pour them into. The ghouls are what got left over.

**They ask you what happens after the party.**

You tell them the truth, or you do not. **Both are available and the game never
locks either.**

> *"Will I still be there in the morning?"*
>
> *"That's alright. As long as it's the right morning."*

Then the room resolves into your living room, and they are at your table with
your mother, and the two of you say *happy birthday* to nobody.

### ACT V — THE 30TH · a party, then a death

A cathedral of printed calendar pages. February is red. The 30th is printed and
still wet, and you can see it being printed by a machine you cannot find.

**It is full.** Four hundred children, all having a birthday, all with your face.

They are not monsters. **That is the horror.** They are children, celebrating
simultaneously, in a room with no room in it.

**To give the 30th a real day, one real February day has to stop existing.** The
only one available is the 29th. You offer yours. They ask if you are sure — three
times.

### The party

It goes small and warm. A kitchen, a table, a cake. Eleven candles.

**The name on the cake is misspelled — one letter. The same letter as the coat in
Act III.** Nobody could get it right. They do not notice.

> *"Is this the real one?"*
>
> *"Yeah. This is it."*

They blow out the candles. They are very happy. They have never been that before.

### The death

**They do not fall down.** No collapse, no gasp, no blood, nothing to clean up.
**They just get less.**

The party was always paid for out of them. They were holding four hundred
children in one shape and a real day has to end, and when it ends they go home,
and they take the part of them that was them.

They are not frightened. They are not in pain. They are not screaming. **That is
the detail that breaks people.**

> *"Did I do it right?"*
>
> *"Yeah. You did."*

**The music box motif stops. First silence in the game.**

### Your escape

**You get out.** This was always in the design — you were escaping as well as
rescuing, and the game does not make you choose between them.

You wake in your own bed. The 29th. Your mother is in the kitchen calling you
down, because it is your birthday and she remembered.

The party has not been cleaned up. **Two hats. Two plates.** She does not know
why. She will not remember making them. By next week there will be one hat and
she will not know that either.

You remember. They are gone, something is left behind, and you are the only
person who knows it was a person.

Every fourth year, at midnight, one knock. Never explained. Never opened. Never
stopped listening for it.

---

## 8. Why this is *sad horror*

**Sad:** every monster was a disappointed child. The ghouls are not threats, they
are what happens to the ones that got left behind when everyone else got to be
*the one*. You spend the game fleeing from children you feel sorry for, and
sometimes the only way past is to say their birthday out loud.

**Horror:** a bad cheer coming down a hallway and doubling into six voices. Four
hundred faceless children having a birthday at once. One passenger too many in
every car. Adults going through motions at a party that is not happening. A coat
that fits.

**And the real one:** you spend the whole game walking toward someone you have
just lost, knowing the only thing you can give them will kill them, and then you
give it to them anyway. You rescue them. They die. You go home.

There is no weapon. There is nothing to kill. The only verbs are **run, hide, and
be kind**, and being kind is the most expensive one.

## 9. Open questions

1. **Should the Act II separation be visible or a cut-away?** Currently you turn
   around and they are gone. A short scripted glimpse is more painful but makes
   the chase tighter.
2. **Is Act III survivable with no companion to talk to?** Nine months of design
   assumed a voice. It may need more environmental storytelling.
3. **Does the player character choice need to branch the plot?** Currently it is
   presentation only, plus the mirroring. Cheaper, and the mirroring does most of
   the work — but it is your call.
