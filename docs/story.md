> **Register: sad horror with something hunting you.**
> **Player character: chosen at the start — boy or girl.**

## Canon — non-negotiable facts

| # | Fact | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The player **chooses boy or girl** before the game begins. | It is not cosmetic. The child in the cupboard has *your* face. |
| 2 | Everything the protagonist does is written **gender-neutral**. | Never bake a gender into the script. Use "they" in all design docs. |
| 3 | The protagonist is eleven and **is never named on screen**. | Naming them makes them a character. Not naming them makes them the player. |
| 4 | Tonight is **February 29th**. | Everything derives from this. |
| 5 | February 30th is the **discard day** — the extra day the calendar throws away. | The cosmology. |
| 6 | A day can only be discarded if it is **empty**. The 30th never is. | The whole plot. |
| 7 | The Boy/Girl in the cupboard is an **aggregate** — every forgotten birthday in the calendar, compressed into one child. | The ghouls are what got left over. |
| 8 | **The ghouls are the fragments the 30th discarded** when it chose that one shape to keep. | Every monster is a disappointed child. |
| 9 | The **ghouls chase you.** Sound-driven, lost by distance, stopped by doors and light. | The game is now a chase game. |
| 10 | **Nothing can be killed.** No weapon exists or will exist. | Compassion is the only verb. |
| 11 | You can stop a ghoul **briefly by saying its birthday** — once, at close range, at the cost of a memory. | Compassion is the only *weapon*, and it is disposable. |
| 12 | Your companion is **taken from you in Act II**. | Without this there is no rescue, and without a rescue there is no escape. |
| 13 | You **learn in Act IV** that granting the wish will kill them. **They do not know.** | The asymmetry is the game. |
| 14 | They never find out. **You never have to tell them.** | The hardest beat in the script. |
| 15 | The mother is the only person who ever remembered the protagonist's birthday. | She is why the 29th exists as a day to spend. |
| 16 | **There is no good ending.** They die. You survive. | Sad horror, not redemption. |

## The protagonist

**Chosen at the start: boy or girl.** Eleven years old.

The choice is not cosmetic and the game never says so. It is the premise:

- The child in the cupboard has **your** face
- Every ghost wearing your face is **your** face
- The four hundred children in Act V are **all you**

**Every line of dialogue and every piece of script must be gender-neutral.**
Nobody comments on it. Nobody asks. The protagonist is referred to as "you."

**They are not brave. They are polite**, and that is the defining trait and the
source of nearly all the tension, because they keep trying to make frightening
situations comfortable for other people. They open the cupboard because it is rude
not to. They apologise to things that are not alive.

**They cannot fight, cannot kill, and cannot save their companion.** They can run,
hide, say the right thing, and be there.

---

## Your companion

**Apparent age:** eleven. **Actually:** everyone in the discard day, compressed.
**Mirrors the player's chosen gender.**

> *"I want a party."*
> *"That's it. That's the whole of it."*

Not lying, never cruel, never raises their voice. **Pleased** to have someone.
They talk constantly because they have never had a listener.

**Performance rules:**

1. **Never more than three steps from the camera** while present.
2. **Never alone for longer than forty seconds.**
3. **Never frightened, never screaming, never in pain.** Not during the chase,
   not during the death. Their death must not be played as a monster death.
4. They ask you if you are sure — **three times** — before accepting your day.

**Voice:** they use *our* words, slightly wrong. "Not down here." They are not
eloquent. They are small.

## The ghouls — *the monsters*

> **Rule: every monster in this game is a disappointed child.**

The 30th had to compress four thousand forgotten children into one shape and it
could only keep one. It kept your companion. **These are the ones that got
squeezed out.**

A ghoul is a child's shape taken too far: head too large, one arm longer, joints
that bend a direction they shouldn't, a face squeezed flat, still wearing a party
hat two sizes too small.

They are not evil. They are **crushed**, and they are still waiting to be called
in for a birthday that happened years ago.

### Behaviour

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Hunts sound** | Running is loud. Crouch-walking is quiet. Sprinting away is the worst thing you can do. |
| **Loses interest** | Break line of sight *and* stay quiet long enough and it wanders off |
| **Cannot open a closed door** | A closed door is real, physical, and safe. Use them. |
| **Cannot be killed** | No weapon exists in this game |
| **Fears light** | Left in a day with no room; light pushes it back |
| **Can be stopped by saying its birthday** | At close range, once per ghoul, at the cost of a memory |

### Sound

Not a roar. A **bad cheer** — a party noise played slightly wrong, from too many
children at once, clapping out of time. It starts as one voice, doubles into four,
then six, coming down a hallway.

It is recognisably people enjoying themselves. That is the horror.

**Art direction note:** a ghoul is our `wrongness.py` technique
(`art-direction.md` §4.1) applied to the point of illegibility — **4% becomes
25%**. The same trick, dialled past the point where it reads as charming and into
the point where it reads as damaged. One shader, one code path, two settings.

## The Unacknowledged

**Specific** forgotten children — the individuals, where ghouls are the crushed
fragments. Furious, because your companion is getting a wish and they are not.

They flicker: solid peripherally, gone head-on.

> **"Is this my birthday?"**

**"Yes"** → they accept it, go quiet, are gone.
**"No"**, silence, or running → they take it. You lose a memory of your own
birthday.

**There is never enough of you to go around, and the game never tells you that
ignoring them all would have been fine.**

---

## The Late — *Act III*

Children who will not leave a waiting room. They do not chase and do not
threaten. They **wait**, and the longer you look, the less like children they
are.

The only way past is to go to each one and say the thing nobody ever said to them.
It takes a long time. It is worth it.

---

## The Almost — *Act IV*

Translucent adults going through the motions of a party that is not happening.
Not a threat. **The people who almost came.** The saddest thing in the game.

---

## The Guests — *Act I*

Motionless adults facing the street. The parents from every party that ended
badly. They never move, never turn, never pursue. They notice you, and that is
all.

---

## Your mother

**Never named. Never given a cutscene. Three appearances.**

She is the reason tonight is happening.

| Act | Where | What she does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | A garden, among the motionless Guests, facing the street | Nothing. Players routinely miss her. |
| II | Driving the car that takes your companion | *"We'll be there soon."* Does not look at you. |
| IV | Your kitchen table | Both of you say *happy birthday* to nobody. |

**Never rescued. Never explained.** She stays one floor away for the whole game.
A fourth appearance is the easiest possible way to ruin this game.

## Tone rules

1. **Fair tells, always.** Every jumpscare has ≥ 700 ms of sound or visual
   warning. Frightening, not cheap.
2. **Every monster is a disappointed child.** No creatures, no adult-monsters,
   no gore, no body horror. Ever.
3. **Nothing can be killed.** No weapon, no combat verb, no drop.
4. **The companion is never a monster and never frightened** — including in their
   death. No reaching, no screaming, no agony.
5. **Running away from a disappointed child must feel bad.** It is the whole
   point. Do not tune the ghouls to be fun to outrun.
6. **The horror is accuracy.** Every disturbing image is something real and
   slightly wrong.
7. **The settings stay domestic.** Bedroom, hallway, cloakroom, waiting room,
   kitchen.
8. **Never tell the player they were dreaming.**
9. **Never explain the extra passenger.**
10. **The mother is never rescued.**
11. **The player can always tell their companion the truth in Act IV.** Never
    locked.
