# World Map (v3)

> Five acts. The Boy is beside you the whole way through. Every threat is a
> disappointed child and nothing can be killed.

**One night. The whole game is the four seconds after midnight.**

---

## The companion rule

The Boy walks with the player from Act I to Act V. This is not set dressing —
it changes every scene.

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| He **follows**, he does not pathfind intelligently | He stops when you stop. He waits at doorways. He is not a companion AI, he is a child tagging along. |
| He **cannot be hurt** | There is no fail state about protecting him. The tension is *keeping him company*, not keeping him alive. |
| He **talks constantly** | He has never had a listener. Most of his lines are about nothing. |
| He **is never more than three steps from camera** | Including in cutscenes. |
| He **leads you** in Act V | The only act where he walks in front. He knows where he is going. |

## ACT I — 29TH STREET

**The party that ended.** Your street, every mailbox with your house number, and
nobody willing to admit a party happened.

```
              ┌──────┐  ┌──────┐  ┌──────┐
              │  29  │  │  29  │  │  29  │    ← GUESTS (motionless,
              └──────┘  └──────┘  └──────┘      facing the street)
                  ╲        │        ╱
                   ╲       │       ╱
                    ┌─────┴─────┐
                    │    29     │  ← the cake is still inside
                    └─────┬─────┘
                          ║
                     ┌────╨────┐
                     │ cul-de- │
                     │   sac   │  ← loops back on itself
                     └─────────┘
```

### Two mechanics, introduced together

**1 — The Guests notice you.** Thirty to two hundred motionless adults in 70°
cones. No chase. Being *noticed* is the only danger in Act I, and it should stay
ambiguous whether that's dangerous or merely known.

**2 — The Unacknowledged.** The first ghosts appear here. They flicker — solid
peripherally, gone head-on. They ask *"Is this my birthday?"*

> Say **"yes"** → they accept it, go quiet, and are gone.
> Say **"no"**, or nothing, or run → they take it. You lose a memory of your own birthday.

**Act I exists to teach this exchange.** By the end the player should have
helped one and ignored one, and felt the difference.

### Also in this act, deliberately

- The **cake**, still on the table. Eleven candles, correct number, name spelled
  right.
- In one garden, among the Guests, a woman in a cardigan **not facing the
  street**. No name tag. Most players miss her. She is the mother.
- The **photograph** the Boy gives you: he is in it, standing next to another boy
  with your face who is not you. He never explains it.

### Exit
The hallway cupboard in your own house. The door is nine feet tall and already
open.

## ACT II — THE LONG WAY

**The drive that never arrived.** Two kilometres of highway that loops, cars
going the other way at intervals.

The **Unacknowledged** ride in the back seats of the oncoming cars. Watching.

### Duck / Hide / Count

Headlights sweep the carriageway. Three states: **exposed**, **below the line**
(behind the barrier, safe), **hidden** (inside a vehicle, safe for exactly three
passes, then the door opens).

The count is the mechanic. Commit to a car, then leave before you're found out.

### Every car has one passenger too many

In the back seat. Always. No light, no movement, no explanation. **Never
explain it.**

### The scare

Scripted. You are in the back of a car. The driver is your mother. She does not
look at you. *"We'll be there soon."* The radio plays tomorrow's news backwards
and one word comes through clearly, forwards: your name.

> **Act II's job:** the 30th has *her* in it. It is not somewhere you stumbled
> into — somewhere that was already expecting you.

### Exit
An interchange ramp that folds back on itself into a school car park.

## ACT III — LOST AND FOUND

**The kid nobody picked up.** A cloakroom of coats nobody collected.

### The Late

Children who will not leave. They do not chase — they **wait**, and they are
patient in a way that gets worse the longer you look at them.

**The only way past is to go to each one and say the thing nobody ever said to
them.** It takes a long time. It is worth it, and it is the mechanical heart of
the act.

### Identify the real Boy

Twelve children, all with your face, all waiting to be collected. Exactly one is
him. Verify against what you carry:

| Evidence | Found in | Checks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Photograph | Act I | who he is standing next to |
| Bracelet | Act II | bead count, clasp |
| Birthday hat | Act II | the name inside |
| Scar | Act III-a | which knee |

**Wrong answer is not death** — it is a scream and a reset of the hall, and the
game never shows or explains what happened to the child you chose.

### The coat

The coat that fits you is your coat, and the name tag reads your name with **one
letter wrong**. He wants you to wear it. Wearing it is optional. The game never
asks.

## ACT IV — THE AFTER

**The adults who forgot.** A waiting room that is never called. The only act with
no threat in it.

### The Almost

Translucent adults going through the motions of a party that is not happening.
Not dangerous. **The people who almost came.** They are the saddest thing in the
game.

### Reconstruct

Six objects, six different children, six separate ordinary nights. Order them
into a timeline. Wrong orders are allowed and simply show a worse version of the
day.

**The answer is that there is no answer.** No mastermind, no tragedy. Six small
failures. The Boy is not a person with a history — he is a pile of other
people's forgotten children.

And the protagonist is in the pile. Four years ago his mother celebrated on the
27th.

### **He asks you**

This is the turn of the game. After the reconstruction:

> *"Will I still be here in the morning?"*

You can tell him the truth or you can lie. **Both are available and the game
never locks either one.**

He thinks about it, and says:

> *"That's alright. As long as it's the right morning."*

### The scare

The room resolves into your living room. He is at your table with your mother.
Both of them say *happy birthday* to somebody who is not in the room yet.
Nothing else happens. The light does not change. She does not look disturbed.

## ACT V — THE 30TH

**A party. Then a death.** No stealth, no chase. A delivery.

### The room

A cathedral of printed calendar pages. February is red. The 30th is printed,
still wet, and you can see it being printed by a machine you cannot find.

**It is full.** Four hundred children, all having a birthday, all with your face
— the crowd the Boy was made of, all celebrating at once because there was no
other way to hold them.

They are not monsters. **That is the horror.** They are children having a
birthday, simultaneously, in a room with no room in it.

### The price

To give the 30th a real day, one real February day has to stop existing. The
only one available is the 29th. You offer it. He asks if you are sure — three
times, because he is a good kid.

### The party

The room goes small and warm. A kitchen, a table, a cake. Eleven candles.

**The name on the cake is misspelled — one letter. The same letter as the coat
on the name tag in Act III.** Nobody could get it right. He does not notice.

> *"Is this the real one?"*
>
> *"Yeah. This is it."*

He blows out the candles. He is very happy. He has never been that before.

### The death

**He does not fall down.** No collapse, no gasp, no blood, nothing to clean up.
**He just gets less.**

The party was paid for out of him — that was always the arrangement. He is
holding four hundred children in one shape and a real day has to end, and when
it ends they go home, and they take the part of him that was them.

He is not frightened. He is not in pain. **He is not screaming.** That is the
detail that breaks people.

> *"Did I do it right?"*
>
> *"Yeah. You did."*

The **music box motif stops.** First silence in the game.

---

## The morning

You wake up. The 29th. Your mother is calling you down because it is your
birthday and she remembered.

The party has not been cleaned up. **Two hats. Two plates.** She does not know
why, and she will not remember making them, and by next week there will be one hat
and she will not know that either.

You remember. He is gone, something is left behind, and you are the only person
who knows it was a person.

Every fourth year at midnight: one knock. Never explained, never opened, never
stopped listening for.

## World rules for every act

1. **No combat. Nothing can be killed.** No weapon exists.
2. **Every threat is a disappointed child.** There are no creatures.
3. **No procedural generation.** Every act is hand-placed.
4. **Every scare has a fair tell** — ≥ 700 ms of warning.
5. **Every world loops.** No act contains a straight line you walk down once.
6. **The Boy is never alone and never more than three steps from camera.**
7. **The music box motif plays in every act** and stops only when he dies.
8. **The mother appears exactly three times.**
9. **The player can always say the truth to the Boy.** Never locked.
