> from you in Act II and you have to go and get them back.

**One night. The whole game is the four seconds after midnight.**

---

## The chase system

Every act after I runs on this. It is deliberately **simple** — three verbs, no
combat, no stamina upgrades, no weapon.

### The three verbs

| Verb | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| **Run** | Fast. **Loud.** This is how you get caught. |
| **Walk / crouch** | Slow. Near-silent. This is how you survive. |
| **Hide / close the door** | Free. The only reliable safety in the game. |

### How a ghoul works

1. It hears you above a threshold that **running exceeds and walking does not**
2. It moves toward the sound, not at you — so hiding is a real strategy
3. If it loses you for N seconds, it **wanders**, and the path opens back up
4. Light raises your effective noise floor — using the flashlight makes you
   louder. A real, uncomfortable trade.
5. **It cannot open a closed door.** Doors are the resource the game gives you
   most generously and asks for least.
6. **You can say its birthday** at close range, once. It stops — for about eight
   seconds. Then it remembers, and it knows where you are.

### The stamina problem

**There is no stamina bar.** Running is limited by *terrain*, not by a gauge:
corridors, alleys, and cloakroom aisles are short and safe. Open rooms are long
and lethal. The level tells you how to move by how it is shaped.

This is a deliberate choice over the usual meter. A bar turns running into an
account to watch; geometry turns it into a decision about the room you are in.

### The rule that matters

> **There is nothing to kill.** A weapon would turn this game into something it
> is not.

The whole combat system of this game is: run away, close a door, turn a light on,
and — if you have to — say a dead child's birthday out loud.

## ACT I — 29TH STREET

**The party that ended.** Your street. Every mailbox has your house number.
Nobody will admit a party happened.

```
              ┌──────┐  ┌──────┐  ┌──────┐
              │  29  │  │  29  │  │  29  │    ← GUESTS (motionless,
              └──────┘  └──────┘  └──────┘      facing the street)
                  ╲        │        ╱
                   ╲       │       ╱
                    ┌─────┴─────┐
                    │    29     │  ← the cake is still inside
                    └─────┬─────┘
                          ║
                     ┌────╨────┐
                     │ cul-de- │   ← dead end at the far end of the
                     │   sac   │      street. That is where it comes from.
                     └─────────┘
```

Your companion walks beside you the whole time, talking.

### What Act I teaches, in this order

1. **Move.** Walk, run, crouch.
2. **The Guests notice you.** Thirty to two hundred motionless adults in 70° cones.
   No chase. Being noticed is the only danger here, and it stays ambiguous whether
   that is dangerous or merely *known*.
3. **The Unacknowledged.** Ghosts who ask *"Is this my birthday?"* Say yes and they
   rest; say no and you lose a memory of your own birthday.
4. **A ghoul**, at the dead end. It comes round the corner. Nothing explains it.
   You run. This is the safest place in the game for it to happen and the game
   still will not hold your hand.

### Also here, deliberately

- The **cake**, still on the table. Eleven candles, correct number, name right.
- In one garden, among the Guests, a woman in a cardigan **not facing the
  street**. No name tag. Most players miss her. She is your mother.
- The **photograph** — your companion in it, beside another child with your face
  who is not you. Never explained.

### Exit
The hallway cupboard. Nine feet tall. Already open.

## ACT II — THE LONG WAY

**The drive that never arrived.** Two kilometres of highway that loops. Cars
going the other way, all full, everyone facing forward, **one passenger too many
in the back seat.** It is never explained.

The Unacknowledged ride in the back seats of the oncoming cars. Watching.

### Duck / Hide / Count

Headlights sweep the carriageway. Three states — **exposed**, **below the line**
(behind the barrier), **hidden** (inside a vehicle, safe for exactly three passes,
then the door opens). The count is the mechanic: commit to a car, then leave
before you are found out.

### The scare, and the turn

You get into a car. The driver is your mother. She does not look at you. *"We'll
be there soon."* The radio plays tomorrow's news backwards, and one word comes
through clearly, forwards: your name.

**Then they are taken from you.**

It is fast. You were running — you were doing exactly the right thing, outrunning
a ghoul — and when you stop, the road is empty and your companion is not on it.

A party hat on the tarmac. No one else.

**You are alone, and the objective has changed.** You were looking for a way out.
Now you are looking for them, in a world that is looking for you.

### Exit
An interchange ramp that folds back on itself into a school car park.

## ACT III — LOST AND FOUND

**The kid nobody picked up.** A cloakroom of coats nobody collected. Much worse
now that there is nobody talking.

### The Late

Children who will not leave a waiting room. They do not chase — they **wait**,
patiently, and the longer you look the less like children they are.

The only way past is to go to each one and say the thing nobody ever said to them.
It takes a long time. It is worth it, and it is the heart of the act.

### Two ghouls in a loop of racks

The hall is a loop. Two ghouls roam it, and they are faster than Act I's. The
aisles are short — this is the act where the geometry punishes running most
heavily, because the racks break sightlines and every one of them is a place a
ghoul can come round.

**Clothes racks are cover.** They are not solid, but they break line of sight,
and that is most of what you need.

### Identify your companion

Twelve children, all with your face, all waiting to be collected. Exactly one is
yours.

| Evidence | Found in | Checks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Photograph | Act I | who they are standing next to |
| Bracelet | Act II | bead count, clasp |
| Birthday hat | Act II | the name inside |
| Scar | Act III-a | which knee |

**Wrong answer is not death.** It is a scream and a reset, and the game never shows
or explains what happened to the one you chose.

### The coat

The coat that fits you is yours, and the name tag reads your name with **one
letter wrong.** They want you to wear it. Wearing it is optional. The game never
asks.

### Exit
The Lost and Found bin. The size of a bedroom.

## ACT IV — THE AFTER

**The adults who forgot.** A waiting room that is never called. The only act with
nothing chasing you — and the relief is worse than the fear.

### The Almost

Translucent adults going through the motions of a party that is not happening. Not
dangerous. **The people who almost came.**

### Reconstruct

Six objects, six different children, six separate ordinary nights. Order them into
a timeline. Wrong orders are allowed and simply show a worse version of the day.

**The answer is that there is no answer.** No mastermind, no tragedy. Six small
failures.

And your companion is in the pile — every forgotten birthday in the calendar,
compressed into one shape, because the 30th could only think of one child to pour
them into. **The ghouls are what got left over when it chose.**

### They ask you

> *"Will I still be here in the morning?"*

You can tell them the truth or lie. **Both stay available. The game never locks
either.**

> *"That's alright. As long as it's the right morning."*

### The scare

The room resolves into your living room. They are at your table with your mother.
Both of you say *happy birthday* to nobody. The light does not change. She does
not look disturbed.

### Exit
A gurney that unrolls into a corridor, which turns a corner and is a page of a
calendar.

## ACT V — THE 30TH

**A party. Then a death. Then you get out.** No stealth, no chase.

### The room

A cathedral of printed calendar pages. February is red. The 30th is printed, still
wet, and you can see it being printed by a machine you cannot find.

**It is full.** Four hundred children, all having a birthday, all with your face —
the crowd your companion was made of, celebrating at once because there was no
other way to hold them.

They are not monsters. **That is the horror.**

### The price

To give the 30th a real day, one real February day has to stop existing. The only
one available is the 29th. You offer yours. They ask if you are sure — **three
times**.

### The party

It goes small and warm. A kitchen, a table, a cake. Eleven candles.

**The name on the cake is misspelled — one letter. The same letter as the coat in
Act III.** Nobody could get it right. They do not notice.

> *"Is this the real one?"*
>
> *"Yeah. This is it."*

They blow out the candles. They are very happy. They have never been that before.

### The death

**They do not fall down.** No collapse, no gasp, no blood, nothing to clean up.
**They just get less.**

They were holding four hundred children in one shape and a real day has to end,
and when it ends they go home, and they take the part of them that was them.

They are not frightened. They are not in pain. They are not screaming.

> *"Did I do it right?"*
>
> *"Yeah. You did."*

**The music box motif stops. First silence in the game.**

---

## The morning

**You get out.** You were escaping as well as rescuing, and the game never makes
you choose between them.

You wake in your own bed. The 29th. Your mother is calling you down because it is
your birthday and she remembered.

The party has not been cleaned up. **Two hats. Two plates.** She does not know
why, and will not remember making them, and by next week there will be one hat and
she will not know that either.

You remember. They are gone, something is left behind, and you are the only person
who knows it was a person.

Every fourth year at midnight: one knock. Never explained. Never opened. Never
stopped listening for it.

## World rules for every act

1. **No combat. Nothing can be killed.** There is no weapon and there never will be.
2. **Every threat is a disappointed child.** There are no creatures.
3. **Running is loud. Walking is quiet. Doors are free.**
4. **There is no stamina bar** — movement is limited by level geometry.
5. **No procedural generation.** Every act is hand-placed.
6. **Every scare has a fair tell** — ≥ 700 ms of warning.
7. **Every world loops.**
8. **Your companion is never alone and never more than three steps from camera.**
9. **The music box motif plays in every act** and stops only when they die.
10. **Your mother appears exactly three times** and is never rescued.
11. **You can always tell your companion the truth.** Never locked.
